Computational Biology · Personal Project

MHC–Peptide Binding Affinity Prediction

Personal Project · Apr 2022 – May 2022

Overview

A month-long, self-directed detour outside my formal domain: could I learn enough computational immunology from scratch to contribute something real to early-stage drug discovery? I modeled peptide-protein binding affinity to help shrink the drug candidate search space.

Using BLOSUM62 matrix encoding for peptide/protein sequence features and DeepAttentionPan for binding prediction, the project won the Grand Prize at the 2024 AI Drug Discovery Idea Competition hosted by K-Medihub — with one of the judges proposing commercialization on the spot. The work also led to a Ph.D. admissions offer from a structural biology professor in Boston.

Takeaways

A month is enough to get functionally fluent in a new research subfield
With focused, top-down study, I went from zero immunology background to presenting original research findings in about four weeks.
Learn the vocabulary first, not the material in order
Starting with a domain's core terms and how they relate to each other builds understanding faster than reading source material front to back.
Asking a lot of earnest questions reads as research aptitude, not ignorance
Asking many honest questions because you don't know something, instead of staying quiet, is what actually got noticed. It's part of what led to the Ph.D. offer.
Public immune-response datasets are still too small for this problem
The available MHC-peptide binding data isn't yet large enough to support statistically meaningful results. That's a data ceiling, not a modeling one.
BLOSUM62 doesn't capture the physicochemical reality of a peptide
It's the default feature encoding for this kind of model, but a substitution-frequency matrix is a poor stand-in for actual physical and chemical properties.
This exact prediction problem is on drug discovery companies' radar
MHC-peptide binding affinity prediction sits at the center of a lot of deep-learning-driven drug discovery work, part of why the judges proposed commercialization on the spot.
