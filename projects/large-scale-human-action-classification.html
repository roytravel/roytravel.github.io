Large-Scale Human Action Classification

Ellexi · Jan 2023 – May 2023

Overview

Before a public dataset goes out on AI-HUB, someone has to verify it's actually learnable. This project validated a 2-/3-person action classification dataset by training YOWO (You Only Watch Once) models on it and confirming the labels held up.

I trained on a 1.2TB video dataset across 8 GPUs (4× A100, 4× V100) using PyTorch DistributedDataParallel so every GPU stayed fully utilized instead of idling between batches, reaching mAP of 80.64% for 2-person actions and 84.65% for 3-person actions, then Dockerized the inference pipeline for official TTA validation.

Architecture — YOWO

YOWO — a 3D CNN reads spatiotemporal motion across the input clip while a 2D CNN reads spatial detail from the key frame, fused into a single classification + bounding box output

Multi-GPU training

All 4 GPUs (of 8 total, across 2 nodes) pinned at 100% utilization under DistributedDataParallel, no idle time waiting on a lagging worker

On a 1.2TB dataset, a single slow data-loading or gradient-sync step compounds fast across epochs. Getting DDP configured so all 8 GPUs stayed saturated, rather than bottlenecking on one, was what made training at this scale actually finish in a reasonable time.

Takeaways

Multiprocessing preprocessing cuts that stage's time dramatically
Parallelizing the video preprocessing step across multiple processes, instead of running it single-threaded, was one of the highest-leverage speedups in the whole pipeline.
FPS choice directly controls whether training time stays tractable
Feeding every frame at full fps inflates the frame count (and training time) fast. Downsampling to an appropriate fps was what kept a 1.2TB video dataset trainable in a reasonable window.
Multi-GPU training cuts wall-clock time, once it's actually configured right
DDP across 8 GPUs only pays off if every GPU stays saturated. Getting that utilization right, not just adding GPUs, is what turned into a real speedup.
Optuna replaces hand-tuning with automated hyperparameter search
Used Optuna to search learning rate, batch size, and other hyperparameters automatically instead of tuning them by hand one at a time.
